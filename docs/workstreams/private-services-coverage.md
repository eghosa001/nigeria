# Private Services coverage plan

Branch: `scale-services-guide`

This document extends the Services Guide workstream into private-provider services in Nigeria.

## Goal

Build comprehensive coverage of **verifiable private-service tasks used by people and businesses in Nigeria** while preserving the repository's quality-first SEO rule.

"Comprehensive" does not mean generating a page for every company name, outlet, tariff, city or keyword variation. It means covering every materially distinct service intent that can be supported by stable first-party/provider evidence and enough useful detail to justify an indexable guide.

A private-service page is eligible only when all of the following are true:

- the provider operates or serves customers in Nigeria;
- the task has a distinct user goal;
- an official provider website, app help centre, tariff/terms document or regulator-linked first-party source verifies the route;
- the page can name concrete requirements, steps, current fee/status and provider route without guessing;
- near-identical intents cannot be handled better by one stronger canonical page;
- the page can be linked from a provider/domain hub and to adjacent tasks;
- volatile promotions are labelled as such rather than presented as permanent terms;
- conflicting provider information is published as `conflict` instead of silently reconciled.

## Current implementation baseline

As of 2026-10-05, the workstream contains **296 private-provider service guides** inside a **527-guide total Services catalog**. This is a growing verified baseline, not the end-state. Continue the sector/provider inventory until each remaining candidate is either published with sufficient first-party evidence or explicitly excluded because it lacks distinct intent, durable evidence or enough useful procedural content.

## Initial private-service baseline

The first private wave adds provider-backed guides in:

- mobile telecoms/eSIM;
- retail banking and fintech onboarding;
- pay-TV account management;
- airline booking/check-in/change/baggage support;
- international courier/shipment tracking;
- private IELTS booking.

The canonical discovery hub is `/topics/private-services-nigeria`.

## Coverage taxonomy

The backlog should be worked sector by sector. Add a provider only after the provider's own sources have been checked.

### Banking and fintech

Target distinct high-intent customer tasks for licensed banks, microfinance banks, payment-service providers and major consumer fintechs, including:

- account opening by account type when document/eligibility rules materially differ;
- digital-banking/USSD activation;
- debit/virtual card request, activation, replacement and emergency block;
- KYC/account-limit upgrades;
- domiciliary and diaspora account onboarding;
- account statements and reference letters;
- failed-transfer/provider complaint routes;
- merchant/POS onboarding where the service has a distinct business-user process;
- consumer loan applications only when provider eligibility, pricing/status and repayment terms can be safely represented from current first-party sources.

Do not turn every product marketing page into a guide. Interest rates, rewards and promotional yields are highly volatile and should appear only when current and necessary to the task.

### Telecommunications and internet

Cover MTN, Airtel, Glo and other nationally relevant providers where official service documentation exists:

- eSIM activation/migration;
- SIM replacement and lost-phone blocking;
- number porting/provider-specific preparation;
- roaming activation and travel bundles;
- data/airtime transfer and gifting;
- broadband/router onboarding;
- fibre/fixed-wireless installation;
- postpaid account setup and bill payment;
- customer-account recovery;
- provider-specific escalation routes.

NIN-SIM regulatory tasks remain on the existing NCC/government guides unless a provider has a materially distinct troubleshooting route.

### Pay-TV, streaming and home entertainment

Cover provider self-service tasks such as:

- new decoder/account activation;
- subscription payment and reconnection;
- package upgrade/downgrade;
- error-code clearing;
- decoder replacement or ownership transfer;
- streaming-account linking for eligible subscriptions;
- installation/relocation only when the provider publishes a stable process.

Avoid creating separate pages for every bouquet/package price unless the package itself represents a distinct service intent and has enough durable information.

### Airlines and passenger services

For Nigerian and Nigeria-serving airlines with stable first-party help, booking and conditions pages, cover:

- booking;
- manage-booking/rescheduling;
- online check-in;
- baggage allowance and excess-baggage purchase;
- missing/damaged baggage reporting;
- refund/cancellation route;
- unaccompanied-minor service;
- special assistance/medical travel;
- loyalty enrolment/redemption where sufficiently documented.

Airfare pages should never hard-code a fare as if it were permanent. Expose source conflicts for check-in windows, refund conditions or baggage rules when provider pages disagree.

### Intercity bus and mobility services

Where major operators expose stable first-party booking/help information, cover:

- online ticket booking;
- rescheduling/cancellation;
- terminal/boarding requirements;
- luggage rules;
- refunds;
- loyalty/wallet service;
- parcel service when distinct from passenger transport.

Do not create city-pair pages solely for SEO unless the route has unique operational information beyond origin/destination names.

### Logistics, courier and delivery

Cover major providers with verifiable Nigerian service routes:

- domestic/international shipment creation;
- rate/quote process;
- pickup booking;
- ServicePoint/drop-off;
- shipment tracking;
- delivery changes;
- customs/document preparation;
- lost/delayed shipment escalation;
- business account onboarding.

Do not publish fabricated delivery times; route/service estimates belong to the provider quote or tracking system.

### Education, examinations and training

Private/non-government education service coverage can include:

- IELTS providers and test types;
- TOEFL, GRE, GMAT, SAT or other major tests when a current Nigeria booking route exists;
- professional certification booking;
- transcript/credential evaluation services;
- private university application tasks only when the institution exposes stable, substantial first-party instructions.

Do not create one page per test centre unless location changes requirements, fees or service availability in a meaningful way.

### Insurance and private health cover

Cover regulated insurers/HMOs where first-party documentation supports:

- policy purchase/quote;
- enrolment;
- dependent addition;
- renewal;
- claims;
- pre-authorisation;
- provider/hospital lookup;
- motor-policy certificate retrieval;
- travel-insurance claims.

Avoid giving medical advice or representing a policy as suitable for an individual; these pages explain provider processes only.

### Private healthcare and diagnostics

Eligible tasks include provider-backed:

- appointment booking;
- laboratory test booking/preparation;
- imaging appointment route;
- telemedicine onboarding;
- report/result retrieval;
- corporate health-check booking.

A single hospital/clinic page is not automatically an indexable service guide. The task must have sufficient first-party procedural content and clear Nigerian availability.

### Electricity, solar and home utilities

Alongside the existing regulatory/NERC guides, private/distribution-company guides can cover:

- Disco account creation;
- meter request tracking;
- token purchase/retrieval;
- bill payment;
- change of customer/account details;
- connection/reconnection;
- outage reporting;
- provider escalation.

Keep national consumer-rights rules on NERC pages and provider-specific execution on provider pages.

### E-commerce and marketplaces

For large Nigeria-serving marketplaces with official help:

- order placement and payment;
- pickup-point selection;
- returns/refunds;
- failed delivery;
- warranty/service-centre route;
- seller onboarding;
- seller withdrawal/payment issues.

Return windows and category exclusions change frequently, so pages need visible freshness and should prefer the live provider policy over old cached FAQs.

### Property, housing and facility services

Potential private-provider tasks include:

- estate/developer application or payment plans;
- property-management resident onboarding;
- rent/payment portals;
- home-service booking platforms.

Only publish when the provider exposes durable procedural information. Do not turn individual property listings into service pages.

### Legal, accounting and professional services platforms

Cover platformised private services only when the user process is standardized and verifiable, such as:

- online legal-document ordering;
- private background/reference verification;
- accredited professional examination registration;
- bookkeeping/payroll platform onboarding.

Individual law firms, accountants or consultants belong in local/business discovery unless they provide a nationally standardized service with enough source material for a guide.

### Automotive and mobility

Private-service opportunities include:

- ride-hailing driver onboarding;
- vehicle inspection/diagnostic booking;
- insurer/private vehicle verification tools;
- roadside-assistance membership;
- vehicle-financing application;
- fleet/telematics onboarding.

Government licensing and registration remain on FRSC/state service pages.

### Travel and hospitality services

Private-service tasks can include:

- airline services above;
- hotel-chain booking/change/cancellation when Nigeria-specific terms exist;
- major visa-centre appointment services when separate from the government visa decision;
- airport lounge memberships and private transfer booking where a standardized Nigerian process exists.

Keep destination/tourism editorial content in the Tour pillar; only transactional service processes belong here.

### Business SaaS and merchant services

Where Nigerian businesses commonly use a service and official documentation is strong, cover:

- payment-gateway merchant onboarding;
- POS/payment-terminal onboarding;
- e-commerce seller setup;
- payroll/business-account setup;
- logistics business accounts;
- cloud/hosting billing and account recovery when a Nigeria-specific service intent exists.

Technical developer documentation alone is not enough reason for a consumer-facing indexable page.

## Provider/source hierarchy

Prefer sources in this order:

1. Nigerian provider product/help page.
2. Provider terms, tariff, FAQ or downloadable official document.
3. Provider app/help centre on the provider-controlled domain.
4. Regulator page that confirms the provider/service.
5. Official app-store listing only when the provider's website does not document the needed feature.

Never use an affiliate, SEO blog, reseller or forum as the sole verification source for a published private-service guide.

## Scaling mechanics

For each sector:

1. Build a provider inventory.
2. Enumerate distinct user tasks from provider navigation/help centres.
3. Merge synonyms into one intent cluster.
4. Search GSC for existing related demand and cannibalisation.
5. Research first-party sources.
6. Publish only records that pass the service content validator.
7. Add provider/category/topic links in both directions.
8. Re-check source integrity after publication.
9. Add further providers/tasks in small source-verified waves.
10. Stop when remaining candidates lack distinct intent or sufficient first-party evidence.

Before the Services catalog reaches 5,000 records, follow the existing workstream requirement to move the growing catalog behind the D1 content-store/data-access boundary without changing canonical URLs.

## Quality guardrails

- No provider-name doorway pages.
- No city permutations without unique operational value.
- No generated "fees" from third-party estimates.
- No hard-coded dynamic airline fares.
- No unqualified financial-product recommendation.
- No unsupported medical claims.
- No page whose only useful instruction is "contact support."
- No source date newer than the guide's last-verified date.
- No orphan pages.
- No broad/full-suite validation for content-only changes; use only the change-scoped service/content/source checks required by the owner policy.


## Split catalog scaling

From the 450-guide milestone onward, new private-provider waves may be stored in additional validated JSON catalog files and merged through the shared service data layer. This avoids unsafe blind rewrites of an oversized monolithic file while preserving the same validation, canonical URLs, source rules, internal-link rules and public service model.
